\begin{resumo}[ABSTRACT]
\begin{otherlanguage}{english}
\itshape
This work revisits a study carried out at the Faculdade de Tecnologia de
Indaiatuba in 2016, in which a trading robot for the MetaTrader 5 platform,
guided by moving average crossovers, performed well in stable market periods but
accumulated losses over the period that includes the 2008 financial crisis. At the
end of that study, the author suggested that models able to measure volatility
could help overcome this limitation. Building on that suggestion, a new robot was
developed that brings together two statistical models with distinct roles. The
first, called ARIMA, tries to anticipate the direction of prices. The second,
called GARCH, estimates how much the market is likely to fluctuate and decides
whether it is worth trading and with how much. Both models were programmed in the platform's own
language and tested on the official history
of the Brazilian stock exchange, in periods of crisis and of calm, always in
comparison with the original strategy and with a version of the robot without the
volatility control. The results show that this control made the robot safer.
In every period analysed, it fluctuated less than both the version without the
control and the original strategy, and its losses in the worst moments were
smaller than those of the version without the control. In many periods the final
gain was also larger. The ability to predict market direction, however, remained
close to chance, and the differences in gain relative to the original strategy
varied from period to period, sometimes favouring one and sometimes the other.
The conclusion is that
the volatility model works as a protective instrument, especially useful in
prolonged downturns such as the 2008 crisis, and not as a way of predicting the
market better.

\vspace{\onelineskip}
\noindent Keywords: ARIMA-GARCH; algorithmic trading; MetaTrader 5.
\end{otherlanguage}
\end{resumo}
